SPARK · the operating layer · data & automation strategy

Automate the pipeline.
Never the judgment.

The end state you asked for is real and reachable: a digest that assembles and sends itself, where your only input is the review. Getting there safely means being precise about what may be automated, and what must never be.

AUTOMATE FULLY

Collection · computation · assembly · distribution · reminders · freshness checks. Machines gather and calculate; nothing here needs your hands.

NEVER AUTOMATE

Approvals · exception decisions · tier-mismatch resolutions · anything sent to leadership in your name. A governance function that auto-decides is itself a finding.

Your role converges to one thing: 15 minutes of review, once a month. Everything below is the path there.

02 · The architecture

How the data flows, sources to inbox

Three layers. Sources feed a store, the store feeds one compute engine, the engine feeds two outputs, the Command Centre you look at, and the digest that comes to everyone else.

One store, one engine, two outputs. The engine's definitions live in the toolkit's own KPI dictionary, the framework stays the single authority on what gets measured.

04 · The roadmap

Three horizons to hands-free

Each horizon is useful on its own. You never bet on the next one arriving, and the format of the digest never changes, so automating it later is a plumbing swap, not a redesign.

H1 · Now, weeks 1–4
Manual, but structured

Weekly: export toolkit → drop into Command Centre → act on flags. Monthly: fill the digest template by hand from the Centre's numbers and send it yourself.

Needs: nothing. Zero approvals, zero licences.
Your time: ~2 h / week.

H2 · Day 30–90
Semi-automated

Registers move from .xlsx to a central register list (same columns, one afternoon). A scheduled job (Cloud Scheduler → Cloud Function, on the platform you already run) drafts the digest from the list and saves it to your drafts; you check numbers, press send.

Needs: a central register store + one scheduled Cloud Function.
Your time: ~45 min / week.

H3 · Day 90+
Hands-free + review

GCP scheduled query auto-lands the cost CSV monthly. The flow assembles the full digest, KPIs, deltas, flags, freshness stamps, and sends it on schedule from a shared mailbox. You receive it like everyone else. Your input: the 15-minute review, and action on what it flags.

Needs: the SPARK mailbox + scheduled jobs approved (the asks, section 06).
Your time: ~15 min / month + actions.

05 · The digest

Designed now, automated later. From day one you send exactly this format by hand, so when the flow takes over in H3, recipients notice nothing except that it never arrives late.

Full detail: open the Command Centre and drop this month's toolkit. Definitions: KPI/KRI Dictionary (sheet 08), unchanged.
✍️
Your 15-minute review, defined: (1) do the four RAG pills match your sense of reality? (2) is anything red that the flags missed, or flagged that shouldn't be? (3) are the freshness stamps all current? If yes ×3, done. If not, that's the month's real work, found in minutes.
06 · Controls & the asks

Trustworthy automation is audited automation

Failure modes, pre-answered

Flow fails silently → failure branch posts to your Teams chat; no digest is worse than a late one, so late beats silent.
Stale feed → digest ships anyway with a red freshness line naming the owner.
Wrong numbers → definitions live only in sheet 08; the engine reads them, never re-defines them. One dictionary, no drift.
You're away → digest still sends; your deputy in Emerging Tech holds the review checklist.

What never automates, restated

Approvals and gate decisions · exception grants and renewals · tier-mismatch resolutions · the quarterly pack narrative to Alanoud · anything that speaks as you to leadership. The digest reports; humans decide. That line is what makes the whole system defensible in an audit.

The asks, four short messages unlock H2–H3

1 · IT service desk: a shared mailbox, SPARK@MoEnergy.gov.sa. The portal application and the register intake form submit straight into it; it is the front door of the whole intake pipeline.
2 · Cybersecurity: the monthly shadow-AI count per the one-row contract (section 03).
3 · Procurement: quarterly AI-vendor keyword scan of new POs, to the spend list.
4 · Cloud Computing: one scheduled BigQuery export of the cost CSV to the /feeds bucket (Cloud Storage) you read from.
Each is a two-line email. None requires budget. All four are on the framework's side of the line, you're asking owners to feed the register they already endorse.

End state: the machine gathers, computes, and mails. You judge.

That division of labour isn't a compromise on the way to "full" automation, it is the correct design for a governance function. The pipeline runs itself; the judgment stays yours; and the audit trail shows exactly that.
