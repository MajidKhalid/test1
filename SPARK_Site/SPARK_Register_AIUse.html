Register an AI use.
Five minutes.

The AI Governance Framework (Clause 22) keeps one official register of every AI use in the Ministry — vendor SaaS, department tools, scripts, pilots, and SPARK workspaces alike. If your team uses AI that didn't come through SPARK, this page puts it on the map. Registered = protected.

Register it now Why this matters
Registered = protected

A row in the register means audits find order, not surprises. Your use has an owner, a class, and a place.

No penalty — the window is open

The transition amnesty is explicit: declare what already exists, no questions asked about the past.

Broader than "AI projects"

A vendor feature, a script, a chatbot pilot, a SaaS add-on — if it makes automated suggestions or decisions, it counts.

You stay the owner

Registering changes nothing about who runs it. It adds visibility, a recert date, and a named contact — that's all.

Why register — in one honest paragraph

The framework's headline risk indicator is coverage: the share of real AI use that is actually in the register. Every unregistered tool is invisible risk — data crossing borders nobody approved, costs nobody budgeted, decisions nobody can explain later. Registration is visibility, not a verdict: low-risk uses simply get a row and a yearly check-in. High-risk rows get a follow-up conversation. Prohibited patterns get help moving to a safe alternative — usually a SPARK workspace doing the same job inside the boundary.

What happens after you press Send

1

Your mail app opens

The form below pre-fills an email to SPARK@MoEnergy.gov.sa — you review it and press Send. Nothing is submitted anywhere else.

2

The operator screens it

In the weekly intake pass (runbook R1), your submission becomes a register row: an ID, your owner name, a data class, a risk tier.

3

You get your register ID

One confirmation mail — your entry's ID and its next-review date (12 months out for uses already in production). That's the whole process for most uses.

Tier 4–5 uses and anything touching a prohibited pattern get a human follow-up from EA or Cybersecurity — as the framework requires. Everything else just… exists, on the record, protected.

Quick answers

Will I get in trouble for declaring this?

No. The transition window exists exactly so today's reality can enter the register cleanly. The framework wants visibility, not confessions — an unregistered use is the risk, not a registered one.

Not sure it counts as AI?

Register it anyway — triage takes the operator two minutes, and a "not AI, closed" row costs nothing. When in doubt, one row.

Could this run inside SPARK instead?

Often yes — same capability, Ministry GPU, data never leaves. If the follow-up suggests it, or you just prefer it, apply for a workspace.
