The AI Governance Framework, running

The Ministry signed its AI Governance Framework in July 2026. This hub presents the framework in its running state — SPARK: the Ministry's governed innovation sandbox, offering a GCP playground environment to every innovator, and AI models approved to Ministry requirements as a service, with the framework's clauses executing automatically for every AI use — and everything leadership needs to know, in one place.

Internal briefing for leadership · pairs with the decision brief and the Governance Command Centre
Operational figures in this view are illustrative and labeled as such
01 · What SPARK is

One solution, two states: written, and running

The Framework is the solution. In its written state it is a signed document adopted so far by its authors. SPARK is its second state: the same clauses converted into a working system — isolated workspaces, automatic registration, controls that enforce themselves. We are following the framework's own instructions for implementing the framework.

SPARK is not an initiative beside the framework — it is the framework, become a running system.

The question before leadership is not "shall we launch something new?" but "shall we run what we signed?"

02 · The service & its customers

An internal product with customers and a service promise

SPARK runs as a product with two offerings — a GCP playground environment (an isolated workspace per use case, with or without AI) and LLMs approved to Ministry requirements, as a service — behind a published service promise, time-boxed intake lanes, and three customer groups.

Innovators in the energy ecosystem

Build solutions inside the sandbox through a Ministry sponsor — the environment they were missing.

Ministry teams that build

A sanctioned lane for their own prototypes, replacing unregistered tools outside oversight.

Every employee in the ecosystem

Adopts published tools after they graduate from the sandbox — benefiting without ever opening a workspace.

Mandate owners — Cybersecurity, Data, Compliance — are not customers but operating partners: their gates are built into the intake path.

03 · How it works

One project per use case, one shared engine

Every approved use case gets its own isolated project — that alone is the GCP playground offer. Use cases that need AI additionally call one shared, pre-approved model engine on Ministry GPUs in the Dammam cloud region, over the internal network only — no public address, no data leaving.

The operating pattern: isolation per use case, one shared engine, and a control layer that runs itself.

The full animated architecture walkthrough lives in the "How it works" page in the library below.

04 · Governance, executing itself

From signed clauses to running mechanisms

Every load-bearing clause of the framework has a mechanism executing it inside SPARK without manual effort. The highlights:

Registration
Every workspace is born as an AI Register entry. There is no path around the register.
Data classification
The classification question is a gate in the application form itself — "not sure" routes to the data team first.
Sovereignty
Models on Ministry hardware inside the boundary; the model server has no public address, ever.
Human oversight
Tool outputs are labeled machine-assisted and signed off by humans; the sponsor signs the value scorecard.
Access
Folder-scoped delegated admin, quarterly reviews, privileged roles automated by day 90.
Lifecycle
Every workspace has a default duration (90 days) and an explicit fate: graduate to publication, or retire and be archived.

Oversight here is not reports written after the fact — it is the system's own behaviour.

05 · FinOps & guardrails

Spend is visible, bounded, and stoppable

Three financial layers run from day one, and the single biggest saving is the GPU idle schedule (19:00–07:00, Sun–Thu).

Budget A — every workspace

Alerts, not cut-offs

A monthly budget per project with 50 / 85 / 100% actual and 100% forecast thresholds. The requester is emailed directly; events stream to one operator channel.

Budget B — where it applies

Honest spend caps

Enforced caps where the platform supports them (managed services). They cannot cover GPU compute — which is why the third layer exists.

The killswitch

Stops machines, never billing

One action stops the virtual machines in any workspace — reversible in minutes, and it never disables billing, so no data is ever lost.

Illustrative — figures indicate scale, not actuals
Prototype scale
Prototype budgets sit in the hundreds of riyals per workspace per month — not thousands.
The unit that matters
Cost per validated use case — not total spend alone.
Scaling rule
No second GPU until sustained busy-time exceeds 60% — and commitments are deferred six months until patterns settle.

07 · Launch plan & decisions

A three-phase launch, and three decisions

The customer portal reaches its audience through an official announcement carrying the page link — phase by phase, so the service matures with each group before the next.

Phase 1 — now

IT & digital staff

The first announcement inside ITDT: the launch cohort builds, and the service proves itself from within.

Phase 2

All Ministry staff

The announcement widens Ministry-wide: intake opens to everyone, and the first published tools appear in the portal.

Phase 3

The energy ecosystem

Ecosystem innovators join through sponsors — SPARK becomes the Ministry's front door for governed AI experimentation.

1

Endorse SPARK as the framework's official implementation

2

Prioritise the escalated demand backlog
